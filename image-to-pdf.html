    How to convert images to PDF

    1. Add your images

      Drop files onto the box, choose them from your device, or paste them with Ctrl+V. Add as many as you need.

    2. Arrange and adjust

      Drag the pages into order, rotate any that are sideways, then pick a page size, margin and quality.

    3. Download your PDF

      Select Convert to PDF. The file is built on your device and is ready to save in a few seconds.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is the image to PDF converter free?

    Yes. There is no sign-up, no watermark and no limit on how many times you can use it.

    Are my images uploaded to a server?

    No. Leafpress converts your images inside your browser, on your own device. The files are never sent to us or anyone else, so it is suitable for private documents such as IDs, receipts and contracts.

    Which image formats can I use?

    JPG, PNG, WebP, GIF and BMP work in every modern browser, and AVIF works in most. HEIC photos from an iPhone open only in Safari. If your browser cannot read them, set your iPhone camera to Most Compatible or export them as JPG first. Animated GIFs use their first frame.

    Can I put several images in one PDF and change their order?

    Yes. Add as many images as you like, drag them into order or use the arrow buttons, and rotate any that are sideways. Each image becomes one page.

    How do I make the PDF file smaller?

    Choose Medium or Low under Quality. Medium is usually a good balance for email and online forms, and Low gives the smallest file. Large photos from modern phones are the main cause of big PDFs, and lowering the quality shrinks them.

    What happens to transparent PNG images?

    Pages are built from JPEG image data, which cannot be see-through, so transparent areas turn white.

    Does it work on my phone?

    Yes. Tap the box to choose photos from your gallery or take new ones with your camera, then download the PDF from your browser.

    Is there a limit on the number or size of images?

    Leafpress does not set a limit. The practical limit is your device's memory, so if a very large batch stalls, convert it in smaller groups.
